About Me

I am a senior undergraduate student in Intelligent Perception Engineering at the College of Engineering Physics, Shenzhen Technology University (SZTU), graduating in 2027 (GPA 3.78/4.5, ranked 11/138 in my major).

My research focuses on the trustworthy use of AI in education. AI now takes part in many educational decisions: it scores essays, infers what students have mastered, chooses what to teach next, and signals which skills a curriculum should cover. These judgments are usually checked only for agreement with human labels, but agreement is not validity. A grader's scores can be inflated by text unrelated to quality, and a signal that looks predictive may be measuring something else. I approach this through evaluation itself, designing audits and building benchmarks that test such judgments against criteria the system under test cannot define. Next, I want to bring this approach to educational agents, so that they aim at real learning outcomes rather than predictive accuracy alone.

Research Interests:

  • AI in educational assessment — validity and reliability of LLM-based scoring, including robustness to gaming and whether scores reflect actual writing ability
  • Educational agents — teaching agents that model learner state and are evaluated on real learning outcomes, such as delayed post-tests, rather than predictive accuracy
  • Labor-market data for curriculum planning — identifying genuinely emerging skill requirements to inform curricula and training

Under Review

  1. Accuracy Is Not Validity: Construct Validity Auditing for LLM Essay Graders Yaoshuo Wu et al. Under review at AAAI-27 Defines the Construct Validity Margin (CVM), which compares a grader's sensitivity to content degradation with its response to gaming edits, in units of human rater noise. On ASAP, 18 of the 27 grader–prompt cells with QWK ≥ 0.5 have CVM ≤ 0 (16/23 on PERSUADE 2.0); CVM-guided contrastive fine-tuning makes held-out worst-case CVM positive on all eight prompts.
  2. Breadth over Trend: Validating Emerging Job Requirement Signals with the Bilingual EJRD Benchmark Yaoshuo Wu, Haiqin Yang†, Yue Wang, Irwin King Under review at the KDD 2027 Datasets and Benchmarks Track Tests emerging-requirement signals for construct validity against a gold standard they did not define. Employer breadth, not trend momentum, identifies requirements whose postings more often carry employer-selected screening questions (modest adjusted AUC 0.636); momentum ranks below chance on the unadjusted criterion.
